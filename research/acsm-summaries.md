## 1. Resistance Training Prescription for Healthy Adults — 2026 Position Stand

**Citation.** Currier BS, D'Souza AC, Fiatarone Singh MA, Lowisz CV, Rawson ES, Schoenfeld BJ, Smith-Ryan AE, Steen JP, Thomas GA, Triplett NT, Washington TA, Werner TJ, Phillips SM. ACSM Position Stand: Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2026;58(4):851–872. doi:10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897.

**Applicability to Julian.** **HIGH.** This is the current gold-standard ACSM RT prescription, replacing the 2009 Position Stand. Direct input to the training program.

**Method.** Umbrella review of 137 systematic reviews (>30,000 participants) across all RT modalities and adaptations, ranging from novice to experienced trainees. Used PRIOR checklist; quality of evidence (QoE) graded per outcome.

### Headline numbers (quoted from Tables 4 & 6)

**Strength (1RM)**
- Frequency: **2 sessions/wk** per muscle group
- Intensity: **80% 1RM** (positive dose-response)
- Volume: **2–3 sets/exercise per session** (dose-response plateau beyond ~2–3 sets/exercise)
- ROM: **full ROM**
- Order: **at the beginning of training session**
- Type: eccentric flywheel RT outperforms standard

**Hypertrophy**
- Volume: **~10 sets/wk** has dose-response benefit; meta-regression plateau ~**18–20 weekly sets** with diminishing returns beyond
- Type: **eccentric overload** (additional benefit)

**Power** (lower priority for Julian)
- Intensity: **30–70% 1RM**
- Volume: **low-to-moderate, ~24 reps × sets**
- Technique: fast concentric, Olympic-style weightlifting, power RT

**Effort/proximity to failure**
- "Completing sets to fatigue (momentary muscular failure) does not enhance gains in strength, hypertrophy, and power, and so is not necessary for benefits to occur."
- Sufficient effort = "near-failure" or **2–3 RIR**.
- Failure may be inadvisable in some populations (older, vascular concerns, injury risk from form breakdown).

**Variables that did NOT consistently affect outcomes**
- Periodization (not significantly superior to non-periodized when volume-equated)
- Equipment type (free weights vs. machines)
- Exercise complexity
- Time under tension
- Set structure (cluster, drop sets, contrast, rest-redistribution)
- Blood flow restriction (when load is matched)
- Training to failure (see above)

**Body-region targeting**
- "Upper" + "lower" with "push" + "pull" (4 regions) is sufficient to cover major muscle groups
- "Horizontal" + "vertical" can refine upper-body planning (6 regions)

**Safety**
- Across >38,000 participants (>6,700 RT), no excess serious adverse events vs. controls. Non-serious event rate not different from aerobic exercise.

### What's new vs. ACSM 2009 RT Position Stand

1. **Periodization is downgraded.** Previously framed as adaptation-enhancing; 2026 finds it not significantly superior to non-periodized programs under volume-equated conditions for healthy adults.
2. **Failure training is no longer required.** Explicit statement: failure is not necessary; 2–3 RIR is enough.
3. **Individualization > strict prescription.** "Individualizing programs to increase RT participation is, from our perspective, more important than conforming to specific RTx criteria outlined in previous Position Stands."
4. **Minimal-dose RT recognized.** Substantial gains possible from much smaller doses than 2009 implied (which itself was informed by only 3 small studies, n=59 total).
5. **Many forms work.** Elastic band, home-based, machine, free-weight, circuit, velocity-based, power, eccentric flywheel — all yield meaningful improvements vs. control. Mode is much less important than effort + frequency + load.
6. **Progression is not strictly required.** "Progression is not necessary to achieve beneficial outcomes, and overload… is likely a requirement only for those seeking continued longer-term progress."

### Implications for Julian's program

1. **Current 4-day push/pull/legs split with each muscle 2×/wk is correct.** 2× frequency per muscle is the well-supported minimum. No reason to push to 3×/wk during a cut.
2. **Hypertrophy weekly volume cap.** Current rule: 10–14 sets/muscle/wk during cut. ACSM 2026 plateau is ~18–20 weekly sets *outside* a deficit. **Action:** keep the 10–14 cap during the cut (recovery-limited) but explicitly note in `training-rules.md` that the ceiling is deficit-driven, not an absolute hypertrophy ceiling. When transitioning to maintenance/lean-gain, allow ramp toward 16–18 sets/muscle/wk on prioritized regions.
3. **Failure proximity is correctly conservative.** Current rule (RPE 7–9 main lifts, 6–8 accessories) maps to ~1–3 RIR — fully aligned with 2026 evidence. No change needed.
4. **Periodization framing.** Don't sell weekly variation as an adaptation-magic bullet; it's a fatigue-management and adherence tool. Update mental model only — no plan rewrite.
5. **Compound-first ordering and full-ROM emphasis** are both ACSM recommendations and already in `training-rules.md`. Confirmed.
6. **Eccentric overload is a real lever** for hypertrophy. **Action:** consider adding a slow eccentric (3–4 s) phase on one accessory per session in the next plan revision — low cost, evidence-supported.

## 2. Quantity and Quality of Exercise — 2011 Position Stand

**Citation.** Garber CE, Blissmer B, Deschenes MR, Franklin BA, Lamonte MJ, Lee I-M, Nieman DC, Swain DP. ACSM Position Stand: Quantity and Quality of Exercise for Developing and Maintaining Cardiorespiratory, Musculoskeletal, and Neuromotor Fitness in Apparently Healthy Adults: Guidance for Prescribing Exercise. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2011;43(7):1334–1359. doi:10.1249/MSS.0b013e318213fefb.

**Applicability to Julian.** **HIGH.** The framework document for the FITT-VP exercise prescription. Defines the floor for cardio + RT + flexibility + neuromotor work.

### Headline numbers (quoted from Table 2)

**Cardiorespiratory**
- Frequency: moderate **≥5 d/wk**, vigorous **≥3 d/wk**, or combo 3–5 d/wk
- Time: **30–60 min/d (≥150 min/wk)** moderate, OR **20–60 min/d (≥75 min/wk)** vigorous
- Volume target: **≥500–1000 MET-min/wk**
- Step count: **≥7,000 steps/d**, increase by ≥2,000 if below
- Pattern: continuous OR multiple bouts ≥10 min; interval training effective
- Below the 150-min threshold still beneficial, especially in the deconditioned

**Resistance**
- Frequency: each major muscle group **2–3 d/wk**, **≥48 h** between sessions
- Intensity: **60–70% 1RM** novice/intermediate, **≥80% 1RM** advanced, 40–50% 1RM older/sedentary starters
- Reps: **8–12** for strength/power, 10–15 for older starters, 15–20 for endurance, <50% 1RM for muscular endurance
- Sets: **2–4** for strength/power; 1 set effective for novices/older
- Rest: **2–3 min** between sets

**Flexibility**
- Frequency: **≥2–3 d/wk**, daily preferred
- Static stretches: hold **10–30 s** (older: 30–60 s)
- PNF: 3–6 s contraction at 20–75% MVC + 10–30 s assisted stretch
- Volume: ~**60 s total stretching per exercise**

**Neuromotor (balance, agility, coordination, gait)**
- Frequency: **≥2–3 d/wk**
- Time: **≥20–30 min/d**
- Type: balance, proprioceptive, multi-faceted (tai chi, yoga)
- Intensity, volume, optimal pattern: not established

### Implications for Julian's program

1. **Current cardio (2× running, ~25–30 km/wk) covers the moderate cardio floor easily.** ≥150 min/wk MVPA is comfortably exceeded; current setup also reaches the 500–1000 MET-min target.
2. **Current RT frequency (2× per muscle) is squarely in the 2–3 d/wk recommendation window.** No change needed.
3. **Rest periods.** 2–3 min for compounds is the ACSM number; current rule says 2–4 min. Aligned.
4. **Flexibility/mobility** — current `training-rules.md` doesn't formalize a floor. ACSM 2011 says ≥2–3 d/wk, ~60 s per exercise per major muscle-tendon unit. The W16 back-contracture episode already triggered "mandatory mobility 3×/wk" — that's at the ACSM floor and should stay.
5. **Neuromotor** — Julian has zero programmed balance/coordination work. ACSM 2011 says 2–3 d/wk recommended (Cat B for older adults, Cat D probable benefit for younger). **Not a priority during the cut.** Flag for post-cut maintenance phase: 1× short balance/single-leg block per week would close the gap.
6. **Step count** — ≥7,000/d is the floor. Worth tracking if not already (low effort, useful proxy for NEAT during the deficit).

## 3. Nutrition and Athletic Performance — 2016 Joint Position Statement

**Citation.** Thomas DT, Erdman KA, Burke LM. Joint Position Statement: Nutrition and Athletic Performance (Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, Dietitians of Canada, ACSM). *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2016;48(3):543–568. doi:10.1249/MSS.0000000000000852.

**Applicability to Julian.** **HIGH.** Definitive joint position from AND/DC/ACSM. Validates and refines the 2026-04-06 cutting-nutrition synthesis.

### Headline numbers

**Energy availability (EA)**
- EA = (EI − EEE) / FFM, in **kcal/kg FFM/d**
- Optimal function: **~45 kcal/kg FFM/d**
- Risk threshold: **<30 kcal/kg FFM/d** = low EA, associated with impaired endocrine, metabolic, hematological, immune, cardiovascular, GI, psychological, and reproductive function
- LEA is **not** synonymous with negative energy balance — RMR can drop to create a new steady state at low EA

**Protein**
- Daily: **1.2–2.0 g/kg/d**, higher end during energy restriction or for LBM preservation
- Per-meal: **0.25–0.3 g/kg** (~15–25 g, more for larger athletes)
- Distribution: every 3–5 h across the day, including post-exercise; pre-sleep dose useful
- During short-term energy deficit: up to **2.0+ g/kg/d** to preserve LBM
- Quality: source must provide ~10 g essential amino acids (~2.5–3 g leucine) per dose
- Recovery: protein co-ingestion with CHO improves net protein balance (Grade I)

**Carbohydrate (Table 2)**
| Training load | Description | CHO target |
|---|---|---|
| Light | Low intensity / skill | **3–5 g/kg/d** |
| Moderate | Moderate program | **5–7 g/kg/d** |
| High | Endurance, 1–3 h/d | **6–10 g/kg/d** |
| Very High | Extreme, 4–5 h/d | **8–12 g/kg/d** |

- Pre-event >60 min: **1–4 g/kg, 1–4 h before**
- Carb loading >90 min event: **10–12 g/kg over 36–48 h**
- Speedy refuelling (<8 h between sessions): **1–1.2 g/kg/h for first 4 h**
- During exercise: small amounts (mouth rinse) for <45 min; up to ~90 g/h multi-source CHO for long endurance

**Fat**
- Should not be reduced below **20% of total energy** (hormonal/health concern)
- Functional minimum ~0.5 g/kg, healthier floor ~0.8–1.0 g/kg

**Hydration**
- Pre-exercise: **5–10 mL/kg over 2–4 h pre**
- Goal: keep deficit to **<2% body weight**; >2% impairs endurance and cognition
- Cold environments: dehydration still degrades performance; thirst suppressed
- Sodium: replace via salty foods, electrolyte drinks; amount depends on sweat rate

**Negative energy balance & performance (Q1, Grade II)**
- Slow rate of weight loss (~0.7% BW/wk) better preserves performance vs. fast (~1.4% BW/wk)
- Self-selected energy restriction can decrease hormone levels

### Implications for Julian's program

1. **Current cutting protocol (2.0 g/kg protein, ~400 kcal deficit, ~0.5–0.7% BW/wk loss)** is squarely within ACSM/AND/DC ranges. No change to existing 2026-04-06 framework.
2. **Per-meal protein.** 0.25–0.3 g/kg = 22–27 g for 88.6 kg. Current "4 meals × 40–50 g" is well above the per-meal MPS-saturation point — acceptable but not necessarily superior. Floor is 25 g; ceiling for MPS efficiency is ~0.55 g/kg per meal (~49 g). Current approach OK.
3. **Carbohydrate target.** Julian trains ~6 d/wk (4 lifts + 2 runs) with moderate intensity. ACSM "Moderate" band = **5–7 g/kg/d** = 443–620 g/d. Current research log lands at ~3 g/kg (~268 g) which is **below ACSM moderate-training band**. This is a deliberate trade-off during a deficit (carbs absorb the cut), but worth noting: on hard sessions, performance may be glycolytically limited. **Action:** consider periodizing CHO higher on lower-body / long-run days (target ~4 g/kg = ~350 g) and lower on rest/upper-body days.
4. **Energy availability check.** At 88.6 kg / ~21% BF → FFM ≈ 70 kg. EI 2,500 kcal − EEE ~600 kcal/d (avg of training day) ≈ 1,900 kcal / 70 kg FFM = **~27 kcal/kg FFM/d on training days** — that's **below the 30 kcal/kg FFM/d LEA threshold**. **Action:** track this; either accept transient LEA on hard days (typical and not pathological in a planned cut) or shift more intake toward training days. Worth flagging in next weekly review whether mood/sleep/libido/morning HR are showing LEA signs.
5. **Hydration.** No formal protocol exists. ACSM number (5–10 mL/kg pre-exercise) = 440–880 mL pre-session — practically achievable, would help especially on running days. **Action:** add a one-line rule to nutrition-notes ("500 mL water within 2 h pre-session").
6. **Fat floor.** Current target ~0.9 g/kg (80 g) is above the ACSM/Helms 0.5–0.8 g/kg minimum and above the 20% of energy floor. Aligned.

## 4. Physical Activity and Excess Body Weight — 2024 Consensus Statement

**Citation.** Jakicic JM, Apovian CM, Barr-Anderson DJ, Courcoulas AP, Donnelly JE, Ekkekakis P, Hopkins M, Lambert EV, Napolitano MA, Volpe SL. ACSM Consensus Statement: Physical Activity and Excess Body Weight and Adiposity for Adults. *Transl J ACSM* 2024;9(4):e000266.

**Applicability to Julian.** **HIGH.** Current ACSM consensus on PA dose for body-weight regulation. Direct input to the cut.

### Headline numbers

**Prevention of weight gain**
- **≥150 min/wk MVPA** (consensus floor)
- Dose-response above 150 min/wk

**Weight loss (without medication or surgery)**
- ≥150 min/wk MVPA → modest weight loss of **~0.5–3.0 kg**
- **Enhanced** long-term weight loss: **200–300 min/wk** OR **~2,000 kcal/wk** of leisure-time PA
- Dose-response observed across this range

**Weight loss maintenance (after ≥5% loss)**
- Higher doses (225 or 300 min/wk) **NOT** clearly superior to 150 min/wk for maintenance in supervised studies
- Maintenance is more about adherence than additional volume

**Modality findings**
- HIIT **NOT** superior to MVPA for body-weight regulation
- Light-intensity PA can be effective if total energy expenditure is sufficient
- No single mode is superior; **multimodal** (aerobic + RT + lifestyle) recommended
- Aerobic + RT preserves lean/strength better than aerobic alone during weight loss
- Time of day: morning may slightly favor weight-loss outcomes but evidence inconsistent

**Adjunct to anti-obesity medication / bariatric surgery**
- Combined exercise + GLP-1 agonist > medication alone for weight loss and CRF
- After bariatric surgery: PA preserves muscle strength and may improve maintenance

### Implications for Julian's program

1. **Current weekly load (4× strength + 2× running, ~25–30 km running ≈ 150–180 min running + ~3.5–4 h strength) easily exceeds the 200–300 min/wk MVPA "enhanced weight loss" zone.** This validates the current volume — don't casually cut running.
2. **No reason to add HIIT** for body-comp purposes. Current 1× quality / 1× zone-2 split is consistent with ACSM evidence.
3. **Maintenance phase planning.** When Julian transitions from cut to maintenance, the data say he can drop running volume back toward 150 min/wk **without** compromising weight maintenance — but only if behavior/adherence is locked in. Useful for the post-cut transition (not now).
4. **Multimodal is the right strategy.** Aerobic + RT combo (current) is supported. RT preserves LBM during loss — already happening.
5. **Step count complementarity.** Even if structured running is ≥150 min/wk, NEAT/sedentary behavior is independently relevant. **Action:** add a daily step-count floor to the nutrition/lifestyle notes (≥7,000–8,000 steps/d, per Garber 2011 + Jakicic 2024 sedentary-behavior caveats).
6. **No rationale to push to ultra-high volumes** (>300 min/wk MVPA) — diminishing returns and increased recovery cost during a deficit.

## 5. Weight Loss in Weight-Category Sports — 2021 Expert Consensus

**Citation.** Burke LM, Slater GJ, Matthews JJ, Langan-Evans C, Horswill CA. ACSM Expert Consensus Statement on Weight Loss in Weight-Category Sports. *Curr Sports Med Rep* 2021;20(4):199–217. doi:10.1249/JSR.0000000000000846.

**Applicability to Julian.** **LOW (direct).** Julian is not a weight-class athlete; no weigh-ins, no acute "making weight" cycles. **MODERATE (transferable principles)** for chronic-deficit health (LEA, hormones, mood, immune).

### Headline numbers (the ones that transfer)

- LEA ("low energy availability") concept and threshold same as Thomas 2016: **<30 kcal/kg FFM/d** problematic chronically
- Per-meal protein ~**20–25 g of high-quality protein** sufficient to maximize MPS (verbatim from paper)
- Slow rate of loss preferred; rapid rate (>1% BW/wk) increases LBM loss and performance decrement
- Higher dietary protein protects LBM during deficit — Burke 2021 cites Mettler 2010 (ref. 53) as the supporting study; Mettler's protocol used 2.3 vs. 1.0 g/kg/d in trained men under 40% energy restriction. Burke 2021 itself does not quote those specific numbers in body text.

### Adverse effects of chronic LEA highlighted by the paper

- **Endocrine:** suppressed testosterone, IGF-1; menstrual dysfunction in females; reduced libido in males
- **Metabolic:** RMR adaptation/suppression
- **Musculoskeletal:** reduced MPS after several days of LEA; impaired bone metabolism
- **Immune:** mixed evidence; some markers of suppression in weight-category athletes
- **Psychological:** elevations in anxiety, anger, fatigue, depression, confusion (Profile of Mood States literature)
- **Performance:** impaired in 3 of 6 RCTs reviewed; slow loss (0.7%) preserved performance better than fast (1.4%)

### Acute "making weight" recommendations (NOT applicable to Julian)

- Skipped — these address weigh-in tactics for combat sports, rowing, etc. Not relevant.

### Implications for Julian's program

1. **Confirms slow-loss approach is correct.** Current 0.5–0.7% BW/wk is well below the threshold where LBM loss accelerates.
2. **LEA monitoring.** During a long cut, watch for: mood declines, libido drop, sleep disruption, illness frequency, persistent fatigue. **Action:** add a one-line LEA-symptom check to weekly review (mood/energy already there; explicitly add libido, sleep, illness frequency).
3. **No need for diet breaks based on this paper alone.** Existing research log already has 1-week refeeds every 4–5 weeks (validated by MATADOR study). This paper supports the principle but doesn't add a specific cadence.
4. **Per-meal protein floor of 20–25 g** is well exceeded by current setup (40–50 g/meal). Confirmed.
5. **Acute weight-cut tactics ignored.** Don't read this paper as guidance for any kind of rapid-loss strategy — it's the opposite.

## 6. Prevention of Cold Injuries During Exercise — 2006 Position Stand

**Citation.** Castellani JW, Young AJ, Ducharme MB, Giesbrecht GG, Glickman E, Sallis RE. ACSM Position Stand: Prevention of Cold Injuries During Exercise. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2006;38(11):2012–2029.

**Applicability to Julian.** **LOW.**

> ⚠️ **Corregido 2026-08-16.** Esta evaluación se escribió cuando vivía en Buenos Aires y hablaba de
> "inviernos porteños" y "viajes a Bariloche". **Se mudó a Madrid en junio de 2026.** Sigue siendo
> LOW —los inviernos madrileños tampoco alcanzan los umbrales de este paper— pero por otro motivo, y
> **el riesgo relevante ahora es el opuesto**: el verano madrileño. El corpus no tenía nada de calor;
> se añadió el **módulo 12 (ambiente)** con `ENV-001/002`. Ver
> [`../assessments/2026-08-16_system-audit.md`](../assessments/2026-08-16_system-audit.md) §5.2.

Los umbrales de este paper solo se activarían viajando a destinos fríos (esquí en los Pirineos o los
Alpes, norte de Europa en invierno).

### Headline numbers

- **Frostbite risk <5%** when ambient temperature is **above −15 °C (5 °F)**
- **Increased surveillance** when **wind-chill temperature (WCT) < −27 °C (−18 °F)** — at those values, frostbite can occur on exposed skin in **≤30 minutes**
- Hypothermia: defined as core temperature **<35 °C (95 °F)**
- Wet conditions roughly **double** heat loss vs. dry at the same ambient
- Cold-air-induced bronchospasm: up to **80%** of asthmatic athletes experience EIB; non-asthmatic winter athletes also report symptoms
- Dehydration does **NOT** increase cold-injury susceptibility per se, but cold suppresses thirst → underdrinking is common

### Risk-modifying factors

- Wetness, wind, fatigue, low body fat, sleep deprivation, alcohol, prior cold injury, peripheral vascular disease, age extremes
- Asthma + cold + dry air = high EIB risk

### Implications for Julian's program

1. **Madrid conditions — no action needed por frío.** Ni siquiera en enero se alcanzan los umbrales de vigilancia. **Lo que sí muerde en Madrid es el calor**, y este paper no lo cubre → `ENV-001/002`, módulo 12.
2. **If traveling to cold venues** (esquí en Pirineos/Alpes, norte de Europa en invierno): brief checklist before outdoor runs/hikes — layered clothing (wicking base, insulating mid, wind-resistant shell), avoid cotton, monitor WCT, expect higher fluid losses than thirst suggests.
3. **EIB awareness.** Julian's profile doesn't list asthma, but cold-air running can produce mild bronchospasm symptoms even without baseline asthma. If he ever notices wheezing/chest tightness in cold runs, that's the mechanism — easy mitigation: warm up indoors first, breathe through a buff/scarf.
4. **Park this paper.** No edits to current training/running plan from this source.
